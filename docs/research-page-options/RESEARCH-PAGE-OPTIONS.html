Research page: where the book chapters go

Sixteen entries still fit on one page, so nothing needs to move to a second page. What needs to change is the order and the signposting: published work first, the chapters in their own section, and a row at the top that says what kinds of work the page holds and how many of each.

My pick: option B, and keep the name Research.

  1. Sixteen entries are about two screens on a laptop and three on a phone. That is not bloated, and a list of 25 would not be either, because every entry stays one to three lines until someone opens it. A second page would only make visitors click to find out what you have.
  2. Today a visitor sees three preprints before the first published paper, and on a phone the Journal Publications heading sits at the bottom of the first screen. Published work should come first.
  3. The row of buttons with counts tells a visitor what is on the page and how much before they scroll, which is the signal you asked about. A search committee member can tap Journal publications and see only those. A Books button appears by itself the day a book is added.
  4. Keep Research as the menu name. It sits next to Teaching, which also names an activity, and it covers preprints, which are not publications yet. Changing it also changes the page title Google shows, and moving the address would need a forwarding page so old links keep working.
  5. You do not need shadcn. The site does not use shadcn components; the button row would be written in the site's own styles (the same coral, borders and type) and would behave like shadcn's toggle group: arrow keys move between buttons and screen readers announce which one is on. No new package.

What changes

A row of buttons under the title: All, Journal publications, Book chapters, Preprints, each with its count. All shows every section in order; a button shows only that kind. The row stays under the menu bar while you scroll.

What it does well

  • The counts say what is on the page before anyone scrolls.
  • One tap narrows the list to what a visitor came for.
  • A Books button appears by itself when the first book is added.
  • Works the same on a phone, where it is one line tall.

What it costs

  • One more element at the top of the page.
  • On a phone the row takes one line of the screen while you scroll.
  • Once a Books button is added, the row on a phone is slightly wider than the screen and scrolls sideways (try the book preview above).

What changes

The page is renamed Publications. Everything is listed newest first under year headings, with a small tag saying what each entry is. Preprints go last because they have no year. The filter row narrows by kind.

What it does well

  • Shows a steady record over the years.
  • The tags make a chapter easy to tell from an article.

What it costs

  • Renaming changes the menu, the page title and possibly the address.
  • The year headings split the eleven articles into six small groups.
  • The Forthcoming group mixes articles and chapters, so the most important work is not grouped by kind.

What changes

On a laptop, a narrow list on the left names each section with its count and highlights the section you are reading. On a phone it becomes a row of buttons at the top.

What it does well

  • Always visible on a laptop.
  • Grows well if the page later adds books, reports or talks.

What it costs

  • The most code of the five.
  • The page is too short today to need it.
  • It changes the page from one column to two on a laptop.
